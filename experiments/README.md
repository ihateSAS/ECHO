# Experiments

> **Reading note.** These are chronological lab records. They preserve the
> interpretation made after each run, including interpretations that were
> later corrected by the off-grid audit or by inspection of the LoRA target
> modules. The paper and the newest relevant experiment note are authoritative
> when an older entry conflicts with them.

Every training run, backbone comparison, or anything else that generates a
real result belongs here, not just in a chat log or a Colab notebook that
someone will eventually lose. This directory is the permanent record.

## Convention

One folder per experiment: `YYYY-MM-DD-short-slug/`. Each folder has:

- **`notes.md`**: what was run, what happened, and what it means. Written
  for a human to read later with zero other context. State the actual
  command, not just a description of it, so it's rerunnable.
- **`result.json`** (when there's structured output worth keeping, e.g. a
  `VerificationResult`): the raw data behind the notes, so a claim in
  `notes.md` can be checked rather than trusted.

No audio, no model weights, no large artifacts, same rule as the rest of
the repo's `.gitignore`. This is for records small enough to commit:
commands, transcripts, numbers, conclusions.

| 2026-08-10 | `audio-only-stereo-perception` | **The pivotal experiment: can the model localize from audio, not from cues handed to it as text?** No. Fed the two channels as two audio clips with zero cue text, side accuracy is **28.5%** over 1,045 items, *below* the 45.5% always-guess-majority baseline, vs 71.8% when the cues are in the prompt. The confusion matrix is decisive: on truly-right sources the model says "left" (203) 3x more than "right" (65), anti-correlated, not just at chance, and it almost never says "right" at all. So the 71.8% was the text cues doing all the work; the audio contributes nothing usable. This is the **perceptual-failure** half of the two-failure story (perception: can't hear space, this plus the mono encoder; reasoning: unfaithful arithmetic given cues, the earlier runs). Caveat: one model, two-clip method forced by the mono encoder; multi-model sweep is what makes it a field claim. |

| 2026-08-11 | `multimodel-comparison` | **A third model, complete for both conditions: a clean cross-model gradient in reasoning, and a universal perception failure.** Qwen2-Audio, Gemini flash-lite, and GPT-audio, zero-shot, same 1,045 items, both conditions. Cue-text (reasoning): GPT-audio is the best model tested by far (80.7% within-5deg, 77.8% verifier-faithful, roughly double Gemini's 44.9%/42.4% and two orders of magnitude past Qwen2-Audio's 0.4%/0.4%); Gemini edges out GPT-audio on side accuracy only (93.6% vs 90.4%). The boundary-snapping failure (substituting the panning law's own +-30deg limit for real computation) recurs in **all three** models, at a rate that falls monotonically as accuracy rises: Qwen2-Audio ~100% of answered items, Gemini 19.7%, GPT-audio 1.2%, a clean inverse gradient across three unrelated model families. Audio-only (perception): **all three models fail**, in the same rank order as reasoning (GPT-audio 42.0%, Gemini 36.6%, Qwen2-Audio 28.5% side accuracy, every one below the 45.5% always-majority baseline), each with its own directional bias (Qwen defaults "right", Gemini "left" on 54%, GPT-audio "left" even harder on 76% of answers, missing 74.5% of true-right sources). The model best at computing the angle from stated numbers is not meaningfully better at perceiving stereo position from raw audio; reasoning and perception are dissociable capabilities in every model tested, not an artifact of Qwen2-Audio's architecture. GPT-audio's audio-only run hit a real ~88s/item OpenAI rate limit partway through (no errors, no extra cost, just ~23 hours of wall-clock time). Found and fixed two real extraction bugs mid-analysis: the "°" symbol wasn't recognized (undercounted Gemini's coverage), and a verbose CoT answer's *first* mentioned angle (an intermediate formula constant) was taken over its *last* (the actual conclusion, missed by GPT-audio's spontaneous step-by-step answers), both fixed in `verifier/extract.py`, regression-tested, confirmed not to move Qwen2-Audio's already-reported numbers at all. |
| 2026-08-12 | `audio-only-fewshot-pilot` | **Few-shot doesn't rescue perception, and that's informative.** Small pilot (Gemini n=40, GPT-audio n=20) testing whether 3 worked audio examples calibrate the model to a task it secretly could already do, versus teach it nothing because there's no usable signal to calibrate. Gemini got *worse* (35.0% -> 27.5%, both below its own subset's 42.5% majority baseline) and collapsed toward "center", the exact answer given by one of the three exemplars, a surface pattern-match rather than genuine calibration. GPT-audio moved 50.0% -> 55.0%, but its zero-shot number on this subset was already identical to the majority baseline (it said "left" on 18/20 items regardless of truth), so the shift traded one near-constant default for a different, still not clearly audio-driven one, not a coherent accuracy gain. | 2026-08-13 | `audio-flamingo-3-attempt` | **Tried, dropped: worst side accuracy of any model tested, and a new failure mode.** Replicate-hosted (`model/providers.py::AudioFlamingo3LLM`), same packager as the Kimi-Audio deployment and the same reliability problems (13 of 15 items needed retries). | 2026-08-13 | `dual-beats-dithering-inference` | **Inference-only dithering doesn't rescue perception, the same pattern as few-shot.** Testing Dual-BEATs' (arXiv:2607.08800) claim that injecting independent Gaussian noise into each stereo channel before encoding preserves the inter-channel variance normalization would otherwise erase. Applied purely at inference to zero-shot Qwen2-Audio (n=50, matched to the existing zero-shot baseline): 32.0% -> 36.0% side accuracy, within noise at this sample size. The confusion matrix shows the same shape as the earlier few-shot pilot: the model flipped its default bias from left/centre-leaning to right-leaning (29/50 answers say "right" vs 6/50 zero-shot) rather than gaining real use of the signal. Doesn't refute Dual-BEATs -- their reported 97% almost certainly comes from models *trained* with dithering present, not a frozen model seeing it once -- but confirms the fix isn't a free lunch at inference alone. |
| 2026-08-13 | `dual-beats-dithering-finetune` | **The real test: dithering during fine-tuning produces a genuine, above-baseline signal, alongside severe output collapse -- and a real correction to three prior experiment writeups.** While investigating this, found that `scripts/modal_finetune.py`'s LoRA config was never actually freezing the audio encoder as claimed: its `target_modules` list matches by bare layer name, and Qwen2-Audio's encoder attention shares the exact names (`q_proj`/`k_proj`/`v_proj`) the decoder uses, so PEFT was adapting the encoder's attention in every fine-tuning run in this project, verified directly by constructing the model and inspecting which modules got wrapped. The original non-dithered audio-only fine-tune had this same partial encoder access and still collapsed to chance, which rules out "needed a trainable encoder" as the explanation and isolates dithering as the actual variable that changed. Result: fine-tuning with dithering applied throughout training (then evaluated with dithering too) reaches **75.6% side accuracy** on the full 1,045 held-out items, well above the 45.5% majority baseline and the non-dithered fine-tune's chance-level 44.4%. But the raw output is severely degenerate: literally two fixed sentences across all 1,045 answers ("The cello is at 5.0 degrees to the left/right"), wrong instrument and wrong magnitude essentially always, never says "centre". The confusion matrix rules out simple default-guessing though: 99.6% correct on true-left, 66.7% correct on true-right (not one side dominating regardless of truth), a real above-chance binary signal, not the same kind of collapse the non-dithered run showed. Real, independent, cross-architecture support for Dual-BEATs' actual causal claim -- falls well short of their 97.2% ceiling, and needs a repeat with a different seed before trusting it as reproducible, not a lucky draw. **Follow-up in the same folder: unfreezing the rest of the encoder (its output projection, MLP, and normalization layers, on top of dithering) is a regression, not an improvement.** Same training budget (5,332 examples, 1 epoch), more trainable parameters (43.9M -> 51.9M): total collapse to a single constant answer across all 1,045 items ("The cello is at 5.0 degrees to the right", every time), side accuracy exactly at the 45.5% majority baseline, the same shape of failure as the original non-dithered fine-tune, just more extreme (100% constant vs. that run's 91%). More capacity on the same small training budget didn't refine the crude-but-real directional signal into something richer; it erased it. **Second follow-up: tripling the training budget (3 epochs, full 5,332-example set, 15,996 steps) doesn't recover it either.** A cheap 200-example validation looked genuinely promising (clean, mostly monotonic loss decline to 0.15, unlike every other audio-only run's bouncing), but that pattern didn't hold at full scale -- the full run went back to bouncing. Result: 46.4% side accuracy, superficially better than the 1-epoch collapse's 45.5% (two answer templates instead of one), but the confusion matrix shows it's the same illusion: true-right "correct" 72.6% of the time and true-left 29.5% of the time are both just the model's own overall answer-rate distribution (71.9% "right") reproduced regardless of the true input, not a real signal. Across every configuration tried, the **only** one that produced a genuine, input-dependent, above-chance signal was the original accidental setup (dithering + only the encoder's Q/K/V trainable); every deliberate attempt to improve on it made things worse. Real, but fragile -- an existence proof, not yet a reliable recipe. Total real spend across the whole dithering + encoder-unfreeze arc: ~$45-60. |

## Adding a new entry

Make the folder, write `notes.md` first (before you forget why you ran
it), add `result.json` if there's structured output, then add a row to the
index above. If a finding here is later confirmed or overturned by a
bigger run, update the index row to say so rather than leaving a stale
one-line summary standing unqualified.
